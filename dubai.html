The Destination

A city that rewards
knowing where to look.

Dubai is easy to reduce to its superlatives — the tallest, the largest, the newest — and most write-ups do exactly that. The city is more interesting than its own marketing. It is seven hours from London, sits four hours ahead, and delivers reliable winter sun at a standard of hospitality that very few destinations match anywhere in the world.

What it does better than anywhere is service. Not the performative kind, but the operational sort — the transfer that arrives early, the restaurant that finds a table, the housekeeper who notices which side of the bed you slept on. A decade of competition between exceptional hotels has raised the floor to a level that makes most European resorts feel casual by comparison.

The geography matters more than most people realise. Palm Jumeirah is a resort world of its own, quiet and slightly removed. Jumeirah Beach is more connected to the city and better for shorter stays. Downtown is where the architecture and the restaurants are, but it has no beach. Where you stay shapes the trip more than any other decision, which is why our nine hotels are grouped by what they are for rather than ranked by price.

It also works as a stopover better than anywhere else on the route east. Three or four nights on the way to the Maldives, the Seychelles or the Far East gives the journey a shape, and the flight connections through Dubai are the best in the region.

When To Travel

November to March
is the sweet spot.

Dubai has two seasons and the difference between them is stark. From November to March the weather is close to perfect — daytime temperatures between 24°C and 28°C, low humidity, and every outdoor terrace, beach club and rooftop open and busy. This is when the city is at its best and its most expensive.

December and February are peak. Rates roughly double against the shoulder months and the better restaurants need booking weeks ahead. Christmas and New Year in particular sell out early — we generally advise confirming by August.

April and October are the value windows. Warm rather than hot, noticeably quieter, and rates fall significantly. April especially is underrated: the sea is warm, the evenings are comfortable, and you get the same hotels for materially less.

June to September is difficult. Temperatures exceed 40°C with high humidity, and outdoor time becomes limited to early morning and after dark. Rates are at their lowest and the hotels are excellent indoors — but if the beach matters to you, avoid it.

Getting There

Seven hours,
and no connection.

Dubai is one of the easiest long-haul destinations to reach from the UK. Emirates, British Airways and Etihad all fly direct from London, with several departures a day, and there are direct services from Manchester, Birmingham, Glasgow and Newcastle.

Flight time is around seven hours outbound and closer to eight on the return. Most clients take an evening departure and arrive in time for breakfast, which wastes none of the first day.

Dubai International sits close to the city — 15 minutes to Downtown, 20 to Jumeirah Beach, and 30 to 40 to Palm Jumeirah depending on traffic. Private transfers are included with every booking, and for guests staying on the Palm we can arrange arrival by boat, which is both quicker at peak times and a far better introduction to the city.

Business class is available on all routings and is where the Emirates product earns its reputation. We quote it alongside economy as a matter of course.
