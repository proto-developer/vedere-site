The concert

One night a year,
in the hills above Lajatico.

Teatro del Silenzio was built in 2006 on a hillside outside Lajatico, the Tuscan village where Andrea Bocelli was born. It is used for his concert and for nothing else. When the stage is dismantled the theatre returns to a grassy hollow with a small lake at its centre, and the silence of the name is literal.

The evening itself is built around a full orchestra and choir, with guest artists drawn from opera, film and popular music. The programme moves between the arias and the songs that made his name, and it begins as the light leaves the valley. By the second half the hills are dark and the stage is the only thing lit for miles.

It is a concert that people travel to rather than attend, which is why we treat it as a short holiday. Three or four nights in the Tuscan hills, with the concert at the centre of the stay and the towns, the food and the wine arranged around it.

It works for couples marking an occasion, for families bringing parents, and for corporate hosts who want an evening their guests will not forget. Dates and the guest line-up are confirmed by the organiser each season; we tell you the moment they are.

Teatro del Silenzio

An amphitheatre built
for a single evening.

The theatre sits in a natural bowl on a hillside about a kilometre from Lajatico, in the province of Pisa. Rows of seating step down the slope to a stage set beside a small lake, and the view from the upper rows carries across the valley to the hills of the Val d’Era. At the centre of the arena stands a sculpture, changed each year by a different artist.

Because the theatre exists for one concert, everything about the evening is temporary: the stage, the sound and lighting, the hospitality structures and the roads that serve them. Seating is laid out afresh each season, which is why the categories and the sector names are only confirmed once the organiser publishes them.

Arrival is the part guests most often underestimate. Lajatico is a village of a few hundred people and, for one evening, the roads around it carry several thousand cars. Parking is at a distance from the theatre with shuttles and a walk on unpaved paths, and the arrival window is controlled. We build the timings into your transfer and, on the hospitality package, a private car takes you to the closest drop-off point permitted.

Afterwards the exit is slow, and it is worth being unhurried. The best of the evening is often the last half hour, sitting in the dark with the valley below while the theatre empties.

Allow proper time in both directions. An hour from Pisa airport and a similar time from the hotels we recommend, outside concert traffic. On the night, plan on considerably more. We tell you plainly what to expect and arrange the vehicle and the timings so that you do not have to think about it.

Ticket guide

Where you sit changes
the entire evening.

Four categories, each suited to a different kind of guest. Every ticket we sell is sourced through official channels, and issued in your name wherever the organiser personalises tickets for the concert. Sector names and layouts are confirmed by the organiser each season.

Grandstand

A seat in the raised rows towards the back of the arena. The view takes in the whole stage, the lake and the valley beyond, and on a clear evening the sunset behind the hills is part of the performance. The furthest from the stage, and the category we suggest for guests who want the setting as much as the close view.

Numbered Seat · stalls

A numbered seat in the central stalls, facing the stage directly. You see the orchestra properly rather than as a shape, and the sound is at its most balanced. The category most guests choose, and the one included on the Concert Evening package.

Premium Seat · front stalls

The front sectors of the stalls, closest to the stage and the lake. These are the seats guests ask for by name, and they are released in limited numbers. Included on the Tuscan Retreat and Estate Escape packages, and the sensible upgrade for an anniversary or a milestone birthday.

Executive Hospitality

A hosted pre-concert reception and dinner in the hospitality area beside the theatre, a premium seat for the concert, and the shortest walk from the drop-off point. The right answer for clients, for a group that wants to arrive together, and for anyone who would rather not queue on a July evening. Availability is limited and released early.

Choosing your seat

Most first-time visitors are best served by a numbered seat in the central stalls — a clear view of the stage and the orchestra without the cost of the front sectors.

If you are travelling with clients, or marking an occasion, Executive Hospitality earns its premium quickly: dinner before the concert and no logistics on the night.

Concert Evening

The destination

What to do with
the rest of the stay.

The concert takes one evening. The hills around Lajatico comfortably take a week, and the four hotels we recommend put the best of them within an hour’s drive.

San Gimignano is the obvious first stop: fourteen medieval towers above a hilltop town, at its best early in the morning or after seven when the coaches have gone. Volterra, twenty minutes from Lajatico, is quieter and older, with Etruscan walls, a Roman theatre and alabaster workshops that have been in the same families for generations.

Pisa is under an hour and worth more than the tower; the cathedral square is one of the finest in Italy. Florence is a comfortable day trip, or better still a night or two at the end of the stay. The wine roads of Chianti run east of San Gimignano, and the Bolgheri coast, home of the Super Tuscans, is an hour to the west.

We arrange restaurant reservations, private tastings at estates that do not take walk-ins, and a driver for the days you would rather not navigate the hills yourself.

Climate & packing

July in the Tuscan hills.

Guidance based on historical seasonal conditions rather than a forecast. July is the height of summer in Tuscany: hot, dry and bright by day, and warm well into the night.

Typical daytime

30–34°C

Hot and bright. The theatre faces the afternoon sun and there is no shade in the seating, so plan the day around a late arrival.

Typical evening

18–22°C

Warm through the concert, cooler by the time the theatre empties. A light layer is enough.

Rainfall

Rare

July is the driest month of the year here, though a summer storm can move through the hills quickly. The concert goes ahead in light rain.

Sun

Strong

UV is high all day. Sun cream, a hat and water for the arrival and the walk in from the parking.

What to pack

Footwear matters most. The theatre is a grass hillside with gravel and unpaved paths, and there is a walk from the drop-off point. Flat shoes or smart trainers, not heels. Guests dress well for the evening; the ground does not care.

Layers, not bulk. Linen in the afternoon, a light jacket or wrap for after eleven. There is no roof and no walls, so what you carry in is what you have.

Bag policy. The organiser sets the rules on bags, professional cameras, umbrellas and outside food and drink, and they can change from year to year. We send the confirmed official guidance with your final documentation.

Cushions and blankets. Seats are temporary and the evening is long. A small cushion is worth the space in the bag; a light blanket is welcome on the drive back.
