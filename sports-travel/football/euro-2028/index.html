The Tournament

Twenty-four nations,
and a final under the arch.

The European Championship returns to these islands in the summer of 2028, staged jointly by England, Scotland, Wales and the Republic of Ireland — the biggest event the four nations have ever put on together. Fifty-one matches across nine stadiums, more than three million tickets, and a month in which getting a hotel room in any host city will be a competitive sport of its own.

It opens in Cardiff on Friday 9 June at the National Stadium of Wales, under the roof and with the noise that comes with it. It closes at Wembley on Sunday 9 July at five o’clock, with both semi-finals played there in the days before. Quarter-finals are shared between Wembley, Dublin, Glasgow and Cardiff.

Vedere packages — official-route tickets, hospitality and luxury hotels in every host city — will be announced as UEFA opens its sales phases. There is no pricing on this page yet because no tickets have been released to price. What we can do now is put you at the front of the queue: register below and we will come to you before each phase opens, not after.

Climate & packing

A British and Irish summer.

Guidance based on historical seasonal conditions rather than a forecast. June and July are the warmest months across the four host nations, with long evenings.

Typical daytime

Mild to warm

Generally pleasant, warmer in London than in Glasgow or Dublin.

Daylight

Long

Midsummer evenings stay light late — evening kick-offs end in daylight or dusk.

Rain

Possible

Showers are possible in any host city, any week.

Evenings

Cooler

Temperatures drop after sunset, especially in Scotland and Ireland.

What to pack

Layers. Warm afternoons and cool evenings, often on the same day.

A light waterproof. For showers in any of the host cities.

Comfortable shoes. Tournament football means fan zones, walks and a lot of standing.

Stadium bag policy. Venues enforce strict bag rules. We send the details with your tickets.
