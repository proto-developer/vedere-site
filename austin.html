The Austin race weekend

A Grand Prix that behaves
like a festival.

Austin has become the race international travellers plan a holiday around rather than a weekend away. Part of that is the circuit — one of the best modern designs on the calendar. Most of it is the city, which absorbs several hundred thousand visitors each October without ever feeling taken over.

The music is central. The circuit programmes major headline acts across the weekend, and downtown responds in kind: Rainey Street, the Continental Club on South Congress and the smaller rooms on Red River all run late every night of race week. It is entirely normal to watch qualifying in the afternoon and be twenty feet from a band by ten.

Then the food. Texas barbecue is the obvious draw and worth the queue, but the restaurant scene has moved well beyond it. Add clear late-October weather, a walkable downtown and an airport twenty minutes from the centre, and Austin becomes an unusually easy trip to build a longer itinerary around.

It works for almost everyone. Couples treat it as a city break with a race attached. Groups take the grandstands and the bars. Corporate guests use the hospitality and the golf. Families come for the concerts and the fan zones as much as the cars.

Circuit of the Americas

Twenty turns, forty metres
of elevation.

Circuit of the Americas sits roughly fifteen miles south-east of downtown Austin, off State Highway 130. It opened in 2012 as the first purpose-built Formula 1 facility in the United States and remains the most complete: 5.513 kilometres, twenty turns, and elevation changes that do not read on television.

The opening is the signature. From the grid the track climbs sharply — around forty metres — to a blind left-hand crest at Turn 1, one of the steepest approaches anywhere in the sport. Drivers arrive over the brow unsighted and brake hard into the apex. On lap one it produces the best viewing moment of the weekend, and the grandstands on that hill are among the most sought-after seats at the circuit.

From there the lap drops into the Esses, Turns 3 to 6, a fast direction-change sequence modelled on Silverstone's Maggotts and Becketts. It is the most technically impressive part of the track and best appreciated from the elevated banks alongside. The back half is defined by the long straight from the Turn 11 hairpin to Turn 12, where most of the overtaking happens.

The wider event matters as much as the racing. The circuit runs the COTA Culinary Experience with more than thirty Texas restaurants on site, multiple live stages through the day, and headline concerts at the Germania Insurance Amphitheater — Maroon 5 on the Friday and Post Malone on the Saturday for 2026.

Allow proper time in both directions. Thirty to forty minutes from downtown is realistic outside race hours. On Saturday and Sunday plan on considerably more. We build transfer timings around the sessions you want to see, and we always recommend staying for the post-race concert rather than joining the immediate exodus.

Ticket guide

Where you sit changes
the entire weekend.

Four categories, each suited to a different kind of guest. Every ticket we sell is sourced through official channels and issued in your name.

Reserved Grandstand

A numbered seat in a fixed stand for all three days. You see one sector of the circuit properly rather than several partially, and you can leave and return without losing your position. Turn 15 and the Esses stands give the most dramatic car-on-track views; the Main Grandstand gives you the start, the pit lane and the podium.

Premium Grandstand

The stands that command a premium do so for a reason — a view of a genuine overtaking zone, the pit lane, or more than one part of the circuit at once. Turn 1 covers the most decisive corner on the opening lap. Turn 12 sits at the end of the long back straight where most passes are completed. The Main Grandstand covers the grid, the pit stops and the podium ceremony.

Official Hospitality

Elevated trackside viewing with a seat in the shade, food and beverage throughout the day, indoor air-conditioned space to retreat into, and hosting. Across three long days in Texas heat this is a materially different experience, and the right answer for corporate guests and mixed-ability groups.

Premium Hospitality

The highest tier we offer: trackside positioning on the pit straight, full hosting, premium food and beverage, and the shortest walk to and from your seat. Availability is limited and released early — for the 2026 race we recommend confirming well before the summer.

Choosing your ticket

Most first-time visitors are best served by a reserved grandstand at Turn 1 or the Esses — the drama of the opening corner with a guaranteed seat, without the cost of hospitality.

If you are travelling with clients, or in October heat with a group of mixed ages, hospitality earns its premium quickly.

The destination

What to do with
the rest of the weekend.

Race weekend fills three days. Austin comfortably fills five. A short, tested list of what is worth your time between sessions.

Live music, every night

Across race week the city runs at full volume — the Continental Club on South Congress, Antone's downtown, and the Moody Theater for larger names. Most rooms take walk-ins, but the better ones sell out during Grand Prix week.

Barbecue worth queueing for

Franklin Barbecue remains the benchmark and the queue is real. La Barbecue and Terry Black's are excellent with shorter waits. For dinner, Uchi and Suerte are the two reservations worth planning around.

South Congress

The most walkable stretch in Austin: independent boutiques, vintage stores, cocktail bars and the Hotel San José courtyard. Ten minutes from downtown, and the best way to spend a Thursday before the racing starts.

Lady Bird Lake

The lake and its ten-mile trail wrap around downtown. Kayaks run from Rainey Street, and the Congress Avenue bridge bat colony emerges at dusk through to autumn.

Climate & packing

Late October in Texas.

Guidance based on historical seasonal conditions rather than a forecast. Austin in late October is usually warm and bright, but autumn fronts can move through quickly.

Typical daytime

25–28°C

Warm and often bright. Shade at the circuit is limited outside covered grandstands and hospitality.

Typical evening

14–17°C

Noticeably cooler once the sun drops. A light layer is worth carrying for the post-race concert.

Rainfall

Occasional

Historically a drier month, but October fronts can bring short heavy showers. A packable waterproof is sensible.

Sun

Strong

UV stays high into autumn. High-factor sun cream, a hat and sunglasses are necessary across three full days.

What to pack

Footwear matters most. Circuit of the Americas covers 1,500 acres and the terrain is uneven, with grass banks and long walks between sectors. Broken-in trainers or flat boots — not new shoes, not sandals. Expect 12,000 to 20,000 steps on a full day.

Layers, not bulk. A t-shirt at two in the afternoon and a light jacket at nine in the evening covers most days. Add a packable waterproof shell and you are ready for a front moving through.

Bag policy. The circuit typically operates a clear-bag policy with a size limit, alongside a small permitted clutch. Restrictions on bags, professional cameras, umbrellas and outside food and drink are set by the circuit and can change year to year — we send the confirmed official guidance with your final documentation.

Ear protection. Worth having for anyone sensitive to noise, and essential for children. Available on site but cheaper brought with you.
